Force Capability Workbooks · experimental force design

FCW Builder

Turns an FDW and its sources into a staff-ready Force Capability Workbook. Every claim is labeled, cited, or marked as a gap, and a quality gate decides when the draft is ready for staff review. It runs today. The next step is standing it up on Army systems, starting with Vantage.

Source authority

Four tiers: user direction, approved or final, draft or validated, supporting reference. Tier 5 is reserved for labeled assumptions. When sources conflict, the higher tier controls. If no tier controls, the conflict becomes an open issue instead of a guess.

Labels and gap markers

A Source-supported claim without a citation fails the gate.

Quality gate and staff review

The nine checks from the module's Final Quality Gate, plus one autonomy check (A1). Authors submit; a reviewer who is not the author marks the workbook reviewed or returns it with a comment.

Army Autonomous Initiatives

The 01 Oct 2026 memo is loaded as a campaign. The six formations (aviation, armor, fires, sustainment, engineer, training) are workbook slots. The five FY28 priority lines become cited capability rows. Each autonomous capability carries a human in, on, or over the loop tag, and check A1 enforces it (DoDD 3000.09).

Outputs: the FCW draft in the module's required structure, brief slides generated from the workbook, line-and-wire org YAML that renders to editable PowerPoint, and a Vantage package (ontology manifest, AI FDE prompt, one dataset per object type).

Live demo

Try it in ten minutes

  1. Open the live demo. The register shows the Army Autonomous Initiatives board with the six formations from the memo, and the two worked examples, Corps 2040 and Division 2040.

  2. Under Aviation, choose Start workbook, give it a name, and create it. The memo and DoDD 3000.09 are linked as sources, and the entries the memo states outright are already cited to their paragraphs.

  3. On Sources, run the source assessment. Each source conflict it finds has a Resolve block: the higher tier controls, or you record an open issue. The ✕ on a source row removes it.

  4. On COAs, choose Open COA workbench: rank the COAs best to worst on each criterion, edit or add COAs and their wiring sketches, build a hybrid, and select the one the design carries.

  5. Open Design, then section 1.3. Add the priority lines that apply, select a row, and set its human-control level in the right-hand panel.

  6. Open Gate to see what blocks staff review, then Brief for the directive and autonomy slides and an OV-1 drawn from the workbook.

  7. For a finished design, open Corps 2040 or Division 2040. Both carry a full source register, scored COAs, a recommended hybrid design, and an interactive OV-1: pass control between command nodes, cut the network with DDIL, and on the division swap the DCG roles.

  8. Still on Design, open OV-1 and switch to 3D operational view: orbit the concept of employment across stacked domain planes, switch the framework lens (2026, HYBRID, 2040 EXP), step through the 2.3 phases (each names the decision to test), cut the network with DDIL, pass control, and click any label for its source. It is also a slide in the brief, and the Export menu gives the scene specification as JSON.

  9. Back on the register, choose Present findings on Corps 2040 or Division 2040. The brief opens on the operational-environment slide: why a dispersed, machine-speed headquarters is required, the research findings, and the evidence base. Arrow keys change slides. Every addition from the research pass is an AI proposal until a human accepts it in the workspace.

The banner reads AI generated · Not yet validated by human eyes because the examples carry an AI research pass that no one has reviewed yet. The demo runs entirely in your browser; nothing you enter leaves it. On its own it has no language model, so drafting adds the structure and marks every gap [TBD]. If you run Ollama with an open-weight model such as Gemma 4, open the backend badge in the header and choose Connect. The agent, the source assessment, and drafting then run on your own computer, as proposals you accept. First allow this site in Ollama: run launchctl setenv OLLAMA_ORIGINS "https://srg13640.github.io" on a Mac, then quit and reopen Ollama. On the team server, or with AIP Logic in Vantage, the model reads the source documents themselves. Downloads and printing are off in the hosted demo.

Ground rules

Built in, not optional

Markings

The builder never sets a classification marking. The originator marks every exported product.

Content

No CUI goes on GitHub or in this demo. The two worked examples are experimental designs from an analyst draft and public sources, not approved organizations; personal names are removed.

The agent proposes

The author applies. The agent never chooses a human-control level or decides a conflict that no tier controls.

Every write is an action

Each change runs a named action with submission criteria, the same ones Vantage enforces, and is logged.
